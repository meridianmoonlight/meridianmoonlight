## Status: this is a proposal. No code has been written yet.

What exists today is a design, an auditable compute model, and a whitepaper. What does not exist is an app, a coordinator, or a network. Milestone 0 is one phone answering one question — and it hasn't happened.

We are publishing at this stage on purpose. The numbers below are the ones most likely to be wrong, and it is cheaper to be corrected now than after a year of building on a bad assumption.

---

## The idea

Roughly 1.2 billion smartphones have enough memory to run a modern small language model. Almost all of them spend the night on a charger, doing nothing.

Meridian Moonlight turns willing participants' phones into nodes of a free AI network:

- **Every phone runs a complete model, never a fragment.** Splitting one model across the internet fails on latency physics ([why](WHITEPAPER.md#21-this-is-not-one-giant-brain)). We don't try.
- **Contribution is gated to conditions you'd never notice** — plugged in, on Wi-Fi, screen off, battery above 80%, device cool. One switch turns it off forever.
- **Compute follows the moon.** Night circles the planet continuously, so supply migrates westward around the clock and never falls below ~14% of the fleet.
- **Requests are answered locally first.** Your prompts don't leave your device unless they have to.

## What makes this repo different: we retract our own headline claim

An earlier draft of this project claimed that **1.4% adoption (~30M phones) would surpass the largest AI data center on Earth.** That claim was built on peak NPU throughput figures. It does not survive a bandwidth-bound analysis, and it is wrong by roughly 400×.

**LLM decoding on a phone is limited by memory bandwidth, not arithmetic.** Generating one token requires reading every model weight out of RAM. A flagship advertising 45 INT8 TOPS sustains about **0.098 TOPS** on a 3B model — a **457× gap** between the number on the box and the number that serves a user.

![The bandwidth wall](docs/figures/fig1_bandwidth_wall.png)

So the network will not out-compute a hyperscale data center in raw operations. Not at 30 million devices, not at any number of devices. [Full retraction table](WHITEPAPER.md#appendix-b-what-we-retract).

### What survives is a better thesis

**The network doesn't need to beat a data center. It needs to serve the people in it — and it does that from the first thousand devices.**

Capacity and membership grow together, so each participant's share is about **274,000 output tokens per day** — roughly **9× heavy personal use**, and 5× even at the daily supply trough. That ratio is identical at one thousand devices and at one billion. There is no critical mass to reach, and no chicken-and-egg problem that needs a token to solve.

![Supply and demand grow together](docs/figures/fig2_self_sufficiency.png)

The ~89% surplus above member use is a research instrument:

| Enrolled devices | Sustained FP32 | vs Folding@home at its peak |
|---|---|---|
| 1 million | 0.08 exaFLOPS | 0.03× |
| **31 million** | **2.4 exaFLOPS** | **1.0× — parity** |
| 100 million | 7.7 exaFLOPS | 3.2× |
| 1.2 billion (every capable phone) | **92 exaFLOPS** | **38×** |

At full enrolment, about **ten days** of the network delivers what Folding@home delivered in a year at its COVID-era peak. That's ~30× smaller than the previous draft claimed, and it's defensible.

## Check our math — please

Every number in the whitepaper, this README, and the website is generated by one auditable script. Nothing is hand-typed downstream.

```bash
pip install numpy matplotlib
python analysis/compute_model.py
```

That writes [`analysis/NUMBERS.md`](analysis/NUMBERS.md), [`analysis/numbers.json`](analysis/numbers.json), and every figure in `docs/figures/`. Assumptions are named constants at the top of [`compute_model.py`](analysis/compute_model.py) with their basis and confidence stated inline.

**Finding an error here is the single most useful contribution you can make.** [Open an issue](../../issues/new) — including for the assumptions we've flagged as low-confidence ourselves.

The [sensitivity analysis](WHITEPAPER.md#7-sensitivity-what-if-we-are-wrong) shows the full range across every assumption is 16M–63M devices for the Folding@home-parity claim. A 4× band, published up front.

## Honest limits

Three of these are physics. They will not be engineered away.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Not one giant brain** | Splitting a model across internet-connected phones costs a network round trip per layer per token. Five to six orders of magnitude too slow. Each node runs a whole model. |
| **Will not train frontier models** | Training needs terabytes/second between processors inches apart. Federated *fine-tuning* of a small model is in scope; pre-training never will be. |
| **Throughput, not a pooled engine** | A million independent tasks, not one task a million times faster. A million couriers, not one cargo jet. |
| **Data centers batch; phones can't** | A data center reads each weight once and serves hundreds of concurrent users with it. A phone reads it once per token for one user. Per unit of hardware, a data center is far more efficient at inference. Our advantage is that the hardware is already bought and the marginal cost is zero. |

## Open questions we haven't settled

These are flagged as unresolved rather than papered over. Opinions welcome.

1. **Whose content runs on whose phone?** ([§9.2](WHITEPAPER.md#92-the-hard-problem-whose-content-runs-on-whose-phone)) Routing a stranger's prompt to a volunteer's device puts legal exposure on that volunteer. Current recommendation: **inference stays local-only; the network carries only vetted institutional batch work** through M2. That defers the most emotionally compelling part of the pitch. It may be the wrong call.
2. **Funding.** No institution has agreed to pay for anything. M0–M2 must be affordable to run essentially unfunded.
3. **Battery health over years.** We have no longitudinal data and won't for a year. Currently an assumption.
4. **iOS.** Background execution limits effectively forbid this model. iOS nodes will be app-open-and-charging only.

## Roadmap

![Roadmap](docs/figures/fig5_roadmap.png)

| | Milestone | Scale | The deliverable that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M0** | One node lives | 1 device | **Measured** tok/s, watts, and thermals from real phones — replacing the modelled figures above |
| **M1** | The network answers | ~100 | Routing, redundant verification, reputation, live node map |
| **M2** | Follow the moon | ~10K | **Measured** 24-hour availability curve; first real science batch job |
| **M3** | Open protocol | ~100K | Spec v1.0, third-party nodes, governance in force, first research partner |
| **M4** | Public utility | 1M+ | Full P2P discovery; a network nobody can switch off |

Every milestone ships something that runs. [Full breakdown with issue-ready tasks](docs/MILESTONES.md).

If M0's measurements contradict the model, the model changes and the whitepaper gets revised — the same thing that produced this version.

## Contributing

The project needs, roughly in order of urgency:

- **Skeptics.** Read [`analysis/compute_model.py`](analysis/compute_model.py) and tell us what's wrong. Especially the thermal derate and the FP32 sustained fraction — we've flagged both as low-confidence.
- **Android/Kotlin engineers.** M0 is llama.cpp via JNI plus a foreground service with a charging/Wi-Fi/idle gate. It is a weekend project for the right person.
- **Anyone with a phone and patience.** M0 needs measured tok/s, sustained watts, and thermals across many device models. That's a table we cannot build alone.
- **Researchers** with an embarrassingly parallel problem and no budget. That is the entire point of the surplus.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Be the kind of person described in [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
